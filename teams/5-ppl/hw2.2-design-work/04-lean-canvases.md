# Exercise 4: Lean Canvases

These four canvases were generated with AI for the four finalists in the simulated voting round. Each has all nine boxes. Customer needs, pricing, and possible advantages are assumptions until tested.

## Prompt used for each concept

```text
Create a Lean Startup Canvas for this concept:
[PROBLEM]: describe the problem
[SOLUTION]: describe the solution concept
[TARGET USER]: describe the specific user
Fill out all 9 boxes of the Lean Canvas.
Be specific and realistic.
Flag any assumptions that need validation.
```

Context supplied: the course syllabus and class 3 instructions. Favor simple websites, adult users we can reach, a six-week first version, interviews with 10 people, and a path toward about 100 users. Do not treat those targets as completed work.

## A1: PartyLens

**Prompt inputs:** Problem: photos from one event remain on different phones. Solution: an event link for uploading selected photos into one private album. Target user: an adult student hosting or attending a small campus gathering.

| Canvas box | Draft |
| --- | --- |
| Problem | Hosts chase guests for photos. Pictures are scattered across chats. Guests may avoid another app or account. Current alternatives include group chats and shared cloud albums. |
| Customer segments | Adult student hosts and their guests. Early users could be people organizing birthdays, club socials, or small gatherings. |
| Unique value proposition | Get the event’s photos in one place through one link, with a short upload flow. |
| Solution | Create an event album and QR code. Let guests select photos to upload. Let the host manage sharing and remove unwanted uploads. |
| Channels | Ask adult classmates and student organizers to try it at real gatherings. Let each host invite guests through the event link. |
| Revenue streams | Start with a free pilot. Test whether hosts would pay a small fee for longer storage or an album export. Willingness to pay is unknown. |
| Cost structure | Website hosting, photo storage, bandwidth, and time spent handling unwanted uploads or support. Storage limits would be needed. |
| Key metrics | Events created, guests who upload, photos collected per event, hosts who create a second event, and time spent collecting photos. |
| Unfair advantage | None established. Direct feedback from student events could help improve the flow, but access to classmates is easy for competitors to copy. |

**Assumptions to validate:** Hosts find collecting photos annoying enough to change their habits. Guests will open a link and upload. Existing album tools leave a meaningful gap. Hosts value the paid options.

**First test:** Interview student hosts about their most recent events and how they collected photos. Then test a simple upload page at one gathering, using photos participants choose to share.

## B1: RainWindow

**Prompt inputs:** Problem: students with some flexibility must compare forecasts to decide when to walk outside. Solution: compare expected rain across a chosen departure window. Target user: an adult student walking to campus who can leave at more than one time.

| Canvas box | Draft |
| --- | --- |
| Problem | A broad rain forecast does not answer when to leave. Comparing time slots takes effort. A forecast can change after a person makes a plan. Current alternatives are weather apps or leaving on a fixed schedule. |
| Customer segments | Adult students who walk to campus and have flexible departure times. Early users could be students deciding between leaving now or within the next hour. |
| Unique value proposition | Turn a forecast into a simple comparison of the times you could leave. |
| Solution | Enter an approximate area and departure window. Compare forecast rain for the available times. Show the forecast update time and make uncertainty clear. |
| Channels | Invite adult students through class and campus groups. Share a simple planning page and ask users to try it when rain is forecast. |
| Revenue streams | Free pilot first. Later test whether people would pay for saved schedules or reminders. A free weather app may already be enough for most users. |
| Cost structure | Hosting, weather data access under a suitable license, and time spent maintaining forecast updates. Data cost depends on the provider and usage. |
| Key metrics | Departure comparisons made, repeat use on rainy days, users who change their planned departure, and feedback on whether the comparison helped. |
| Unfair advantage | None established. A simple flow focused on student schedules could be useful, but competitors could copy it. |

**Assumptions to validate:** Enough students can change their departure time. They find comparing forecasts frustrating. The available data is detailed enough to help. Reminders would offer value beyond existing apps.

**First test:** Interview students about the last time rain changed their walk to campus. Show a mockup comparing two departure times and ask how they would use it. Present forecasts as estimates, not guarantees.

## A2: PartyStarter

**Prompt inputs:** Problem: a host struggles to get guests interacting. Solution: a website with short, optional activities matched to the gathering. Target user: an adult student hosting a small party where some guests do not know each other.

| Canvas box | Draft |
| --- | --- |
| Problem | Hosts run out of activity ideas. Guests may feel awkward joining a new group. Searching for a suitable game takes time. Current alternatives are familiar games or searching online. |
| Customer segments | Adult student hosts of casual gatherings. Early users could be people inviting friends from different groups. |
| Unique value proposition | Find one activity that fits your group in under a minute. |
| Solution | Choose group size, time, and mood. Receive a few activities with simple instructions. Include clear skip options and no forced personal disclosures. |
| Channels | Adult classmates, student social groups, and links shared by hosts. |
| Revenue streams | Free starter activities. Test a small fee for themed activity packs only if hosts repeatedly use the free version. |
| Cost structure | Hosting, writing and reviewing activities, and collecting feedback about what works. |
| Key metrics | Activities opened, activities actually tried, host satisfaction, and repeat use at another gathering. |
| Unfair advantage | None established. A collection of feedback-tested activities might become useful, but the activities themselves are easy to copy. |

**Assumptions to validate:** Hosts want planned activities. Guests are willing to participate. The suggestions feel less awkward than existing games. Hosts would return to the site.

**First test:** Ask hosts about a recent gathering that felt awkward. Give a volunteer host three short activity cards and ask which, if any, they used. Do not assume downloads mean actual participation.

## A3: CapClip

**Prompt inputs:** Problem: filming a short first-person clip occupies someone’s hands. Solution: an adjustable cap mount for an existing small camera. Target user: an adult hobby creator recording a calm, nonhazardous activity.

| Canvas box | Draft |
| --- | --- |
| Problem | Holding a camera interrupts the activity. Existing mounts may feel awkward. Buying a separate recording setup may be too much for occasional use. Current alternatives are a phone stand, another person filming, or existing wearable mounts. |
| Customer segments | Adult hobby creators filming short demonstrations or casual activities. Early users could be classmates who already make videos. |
| Unique value proposition | Record a short first-person clip with the camera you already have. |
| Solution | A lightweight adjustable mount concept. A simple framing check before recording. A setup guide with camera compatibility and battery checks. |
| Channels | Interviews with adult hobby creators and classmates. Test interest through sketches and a landing page before making or selling hardware. |
| Revenue streams | Possible one-time accessory sales. Price and demand are unknown; no sales are assumed. |
| Cost structure | Design, prototype materials, fit testing, manufacturing, shipping, and returns. These costs need estimates before production. |
| Key metrics | Creators who want to try it, comfortable fit in supervised prototype tests, usable framing, and preference compared with existing mounts. |
| Unfair advantage | None established. A better fit could become useful, but no original design or protected advantage exists yet. |

**Assumptions to validate:** Creators have a problem current mounts do not solve. A cap can support the intended camera comfortably. The product offers enough value to justify buying an accessory.

**First test:** Interview creators and compare sketches with their current setups. Physical fit and mounting would need careful testing. This is a weaker course choice because hardware takes more work than a simple website.

## Comparison

PartyLens and RainWindow each address a specific task and can be tested with a small website. PartyStarter is also easy to build, but the need may be less urgent and free alternatives are common. CapClip keeps the original camera idea but adds manufacturing and fit challenges. Based on this draft comparison, PartyLens and RainWindow are the proposed top two.
