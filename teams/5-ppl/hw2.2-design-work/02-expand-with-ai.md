# Exercise 2: Expand with AI

These concepts were generated with AI for this submission. Each starting idea produced 10 problem/solution pairs. The pain scores are AI estimates from 1 to 10, not measured demand.

## Prompt used

```text
Act as a product designer doing divergent thinking exercises.
Take this problem and starting idea and generate business ideas.
[PROBLEM]
[STARTING IDEA]
Generate 10 diverse problem-solution pairs. For each:
1. The specific problem
2. Why this persona cares
3. A simple solution concept
4. Likelihood this is a real, painful problem (1-10)
```

## Starting idea A: Parties and cameras

**Problem:** Students hosting or attending small events may miss shared moments, have photos scattered across phones, or find it hard to get people interacting.

**Starting idea:** Flip the floating hat’s party-trick and camera features into a simple tool for social events. Use existing phones instead of floating hardware.

| ID | Concept | Specific problem | Persona and why they care | Simple solution | Pain estimate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | PartyLens | Event photos stay on different phones and are hard to collect. | A student host wants the photos without messaging every guest afterward. | An event link or QR code lets guests upload selected photos into one private album. | 7/10 |
| A2 | PartyStarter | A small gathering stalls when guests do not know each other. | A student host wants guests to feel comfortable without planning a whole program. | A website suggests short, optional activities based on group size and mood. | 6/10 |
| A3 | CapClip | Recording an activity requires holding a phone or buying extra gear. | An adult hobby creator wants a quick first-person clip while keeping both hands free. | A small adjustable cap mount for an existing camera, tested first with a simple mockup. | 5/10 |
| A4 | GroupFrame | A group photo leaves out the person taking it. | Friends want everyone in the same picture. | A browser camera page with a countdown and simple framing guide for a phone on a stand. | 5/10 |
| A5 | ClipTogether | Making an event recap takes time because clips are scattered. | A student organizer wants a short memory of the event. | Guests submit selected clips and the host arranges them in a basic recap template. | 6/10 |
| A6 | PhotoConsent | Guests may not know where their event photos will be shared. | An adult attendee wants control over their own pictures. | An album with approval requests before photos are included in a shared recap. | 7/10 |
| A7 | PropSwap | People buy joke props they use only once. | A student throwing a themed party wants variety without buying everything. | A local board for lending party props between adult students. | 4/10 |
| A8 | FiveMinuteBreak | Guests keep checking their phones and stop talking. | A host wants one short moment when everyone joins in. | An opt-in five-minute group challenge with a clear start and end. | 5/10 |
| A9 | ShotList | A novice organizer forgets to capture important moments. | A student planning a club event wants a simple record of it. | A short photo checklist based on the event type. | 5/10 |
| A10 | AfterPartyLink | Guests lose the photos and details they want after an event. | An attendee wants one place to find the shared memories later. | A host-created follow-up page with the album and event highlights. | 5/10 |

## Starting idea B: Rain and outdoor plans

**Problem:** Students making outdoor plans need to balance their schedules with changing forecasts and different comfort levels about rain.

**Starting idea:** Flip the hat’s holes and “feel the rain and enjoy it” feature into a tool that gives people a choice about their outdoor experience.

| ID | Concept | Specific problem | Persona and why they care | Simple solution | Pain estimate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| B1 | RainWindow | A forecast shows rain, but deciding when to leave still takes effort. | A student with a flexible departure time wants to avoid getting soaked on the walk to campus. | Compare forecast rain across a chosen departure window and show options, with uncertainty visible. | 7/10 |
| B2 | DryMeet | An outdoor meetup needs a backup when rain is forecast. | Friends do not want to cancel or argue about where to go. | A shared plan with one outdoor option, one indoor backup, and a decision time. | 6/10 |
| B3 | UmbrellaCircle | Students forget umbrellas and only need one briefly. | An adult student wants to avoid buying another umbrella. | A small borrowing board for a trusted campus group, starting with manual coordination. | 6/10 |
| B4 | RainWalk | Someone who likes light rain cannot easily find a friend who also wants to go outside. | An adult student wants company for a casual walk. | An opt-in campus walking group where people choose their own rain preference and meeting place. | 3/10 |
| B5 | PackCheck | People forget a jacket or rain cover before leaving. | A student carrying a laptop wants a quick reminder. | A short packing checklist based on the chosen outing and forecast. | 5/10 |
| B6 | CapComfort | A regular cap feels too warm during a walk. | An adult walker wants shade without a stuffy hat. | A breathable cap concept with removable inserts, tested through mockups before manufacturing. | 4/10 |
| B7 | WeatherVote | Friends disagree about whether the weather is good enough for their plan. | A group organizer wants a quick decision instead of a long chat. | A poll showing forecast conditions beside the indoor and outdoor options. | 4/10 |
| B8 | WeatherShot | Creators want rain photos but are unsure when conditions may fit their plans. | An adult hobby photographer wants to plan a shoot around their schedule. | Saved forecast windows for selected photo locations, with no promise that conditions will occur. | 4/10 |
| B9 | RainPlan | A free afternoon is wasted when an outdoor activity falls through. | A student wants a simple alternative without starting the search again. | A short list of indoor and outdoor activities filtered by available time and preferences. | 5/10 |
| B10 | GearBorrow | Someone needs a rain cover or lightweight jacket for one outing. | An adult student wants to borrow occasional gear instead of buying it. | A trusted-group listing page for lending small outdoor items. | 4/10 |

## Filter for the course

A1, A2, and B1 can start as simple websites. Their target users include adult students we could ask to interview. A3 keeps the original camera-hat connection, but making physical hardware is a bigger challenge. These are reasons to investigate the concepts, not evidence that people will use or pay for them.
